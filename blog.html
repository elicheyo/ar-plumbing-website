Drains

5 Signs Your Sewer Line Needs Attention

Gurgling toilets, slow drains in more than one fixture, sewage smells in the yard, unusually green patches of lawn, and water backing up into the tub when you run the washer are all signs of a main sewer line problem.

One slow sink is usually a local clog. Several slow drains at once usually point to the main line. A camera inspection shows exactly what's going on — roots, grease, or a broken pipe — before anyone starts digging.

Water Heaters

Tank vs. Tankless Water Heaters

A traditional tank heater costs less up front and is simple to replace. A tankless heater costs more to install but heats water on demand, takes up less space, and usually lasts longer.

If your tank is over 10 years old, leaking at the base, or making popping noises, it's a good time to compare both options. Armin can walk you through the real costs for your home.

Emergencies

What to Do in a Plumbing Emergency

First, shut off the water. Most homes have a main shutoff valve near the front of the house or where the water line enters. For a single fixture, use the small valve under the sink or behind the toilet.

If water is near outlets or appliances, turn off the power to that area. Then call a plumber — the sooner a leak is stopped, the less damage it does.

Prevention

Why You Shouldn't Pour Grease Down the Drain

Grease goes down as a liquid but cools and hardens inside your pipes. Over time it catches food scraps and builds into stubborn clogs — one of the most common calls we get.

Let grease cool in a can or jar and throw it in the trash. For kitchens that already have buildup, hydro jetting clears the pipe walls instead of just punching a hole through the clog.
